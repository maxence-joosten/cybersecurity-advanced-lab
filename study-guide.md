# Purpose of the course in the curriculum[¶](https://hogenttin.github.io/cybersecurity-advanced/study-guide/#purpose-of-the-course-in-the-curriculum "Permanent link")

Knowing the world of cybersecurity today should prepare you to defend your systems against the attacks of tomorrow. In this course, we will continue to explore the basics of blue teaming: to be able to understand the basic defensive steps needed in every system and network that you will administer. Systems that are 100% secure are not usable or powered down. We will never reach a fully secure system, but not acting will definitely make our systems much less secure. A modern way of approaching cybersecurity is thinking about cyber **resiliency** (assuming things will happen and having things in place to monitor, detect and react).

In this course, we will explore the realms of Cybersecurity. For some of you, this will become a full career path. Others will retain this knowledge in the background of their careers, well knowing that security of the systems is something that always should be taken care of.

This course is one of the last stops in your path of IT Operations (3e phase, 1st semester). In order to tackle this course and the labs, some basic knowledge and skill set is expected from you. Please refer to the [prerequisites](https://hogenttin.github.io/cybersecurity-advanced/prerequisites/) page if you did not already do so. Take the time to carefully read through this page and tackle the material if necessary. Remember that in previous courses you already learned a thing or two about (defensive) security.

## Objectives[¶](https://hogenttin.github.io/cybersecurity-advanced/study-guide/#objectives "Permanent link")

The student is able to ...

*   map the complexity of securing the entire ICT infrastructure, on every part of the system.
*   use tools to detect and prevent attacks on an IT system (intrusion detection and prevention, network segmentation, endpoint detection, ...).
*   perform a risk analysis to identify the most important security problems in an IT system and give advice on how to secure it effectively.

The theoretical content is selected from material found all over the world wide web. This can include, but is not limited to, material on [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/), [LinkedIn Learning](https://www.linkedin.com/learning/), videos on [Panopto](https://hogent.cloud.panopto.eu/), ... .

### Mandatory[¶](https://hogenttin.github.io/cybersecurity-advanced/study-guide/#mandatory "Permanent link")

We provide a set of labs that guide you in adding different layers (cfr. [Swiss Cheese Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_cheese_model)) to secure a realistic company environment (with different networks, servers and clients). They provide a ground knowledge of the domain of defense in Cybersecurity. Each lab is accompanied with a separate theory section. Here, we expect you to fully comprehend, and maybe _study_ a thing or two. We try to provide an overview of what you should know (and be able to explain) and provide some interesting resources to tackle this material. Do not forget you will be questioned on this theoretical material. As a study method, you should build up your summary notes after each moment of self-study. Ask yourself enough questions and transition from a traditional student to a life-long-learner.

We will ask for **your documentation** during the exam!

## Lab work (lab environment)[¶](https://hogenttin.github.io/cybersecurity-advanced/study-guide/#lab-work-lab-environment "Permanent link")

During class hours, more practical hands-on lab material will be covered. You will start with a lab environment, depicting a basic SME network. Class hours are meant to work on the labs and ask questions where needed. The teacher will help, guide and explain concepts or lab material both on an individual and group basis.

Scenario:

*   You are a new recruit joining an existing (company) environment.
*   You get access to "all" systems as a system and network administrator.
*   You are tasked with analyzing and improving the security.

Your job:

*   Take notes about the insecure things.
*   Figure out a way to make it more secure (if possible).
*   This includes in understanding what is not secure and if you take actions to make it more secure, you are expected to know how to revert back to the insecure state. In other words **proper documentation** is **essential**!

Our job:

*   Providing guidelines and materials during lab hours.
*   We are "senior colleagues" who want to help you as a fellow colleague but with _limited_ time.

On the theoretical test:

*   You will receive questions in a theoretical way about the content. This is the theory part of each lab.
*   Note that doing the labs will help in understanding the theory better (and applying it) and this test might contain questions that resolve around use cases closely resembling the labs.
*   This will be an Ans exam.
*   You will have to achieve at least 50% on this test in order to pass this course.

On the oral exam:

*   We will ask you to explain and/or demonstrate your security research and implementations.
*   This will include, but is not limited to, demonstrating the steps to successfully finish lab exercises.
*   You will have to achieve at least 50% on this exam in order to pass the course.
*   A list of blocking factors is provided in the [prerequisites](https://hogenttin.github.io/cybersecurity-advanced/prerequisites/). If you violate any of these blocking factors during the exam, it could result in the **immediate termination** of the exam.

## Teaching methods[¶](https://hogenttin.github.io/cybersecurity-advanced/study-guide/#teaching-methods "Permanent link")

### "Flipped" classroom[¶](https://hogenttin.github.io/cybersecurity-advanced/study-guide/#flipped-classroom "Permanent link")

This course will use some sort of flipped-classroom approach: the theory is explained using videos and material from external sources. Taking a look at the theory before tackling the lab proves to be very beneficial. We recommend to never treat theory and labs as separate entities as they go hand in hand. Finishing a lab will help in fully grasping and understanding the theory, whereas grasping the theory will help in understanding what you do in the lab. On average about 90 minutes of self-lecture materials per week is what you are up for. Contact moments during class hours of course can be used to ask questions on the materials that you have studied, if something would be unclear. Also, the other way round, tutors might ask some questions to you!

### Contact moments (Gent, Aalst)[¶](https://hogenttin.github.io/cybersecurity-advanced/study-guide/#contact-moments-gent-aalst "Permanent link")

Hands-on lab work will be the focus of contact moments, so that you get a better grip on the theory while doing things in a real life scenario. During class hours you should learn by doing!

### TIAO students[¶](https://hogenttin.github.io/cybersecurity-advanced/study-guide/#tiao-students "Permanent link")

The flipped classroom concept presents the course materials in an ideal format for remote students as well: the course material and video lectures are available for who studies at his own pace. Lab assignments then can be pursued in your own time as well. When bumping into questions, these can be bundled into the contact moments that you have during the semester. Contact via e-mail is an alternative if you would get really stuck.

## Evaluation[¶](https://hogenttin.github.io/cybersecurity-advanced/study-guide/#evaluation "Permanent link")

The exam of this course will be the combination of an Ans test, covering the theory, and an oral exam: each student will have max. 15 minutes to present/explain/demo his security implementations using his own lab environment, and will be questioned to review if the student properly mastered these implementations.
